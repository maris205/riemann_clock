Fe II (new window)
Energy Levels:  Nave and Johansson 2013     The present level list, based on Nave and Johansson 2013 , contains only the energy levels identified by these authors in the laboratory spectra of hollow cathode discharges. In addition to this list, Castelli and Kurucz 2010 found several tens of highly excited levels by analyzing spectra of the star HR 6000. These levels need further experimental confirmation in laboratory experiments. They can be found in the extended list of Kurucz.

    Two level intervals within the 3d64s a 6D ground term, 6D3/2-6D1/2 = 114.43838(6) cm-1 and 6D5/2-6D3/2 = 194.92813(4) cm-1, were measured by Brown et al. 1998 using the laser magnetic resonance method. These measured intervals were not included by Nave and Johansson in their level optimization. For consistency with energy levels derived by Nave and Johansson 2013 , we have retained their less accurate values 114.4380(5) and 194.9289(5) cm-1, respectively, for these intervals.

    All Landé factors are quoted from the compilation of Sugar and Corliss 1985 .

    Percentage compositions of wavefunctions for all even levels and odd levels below 106000 cm-1 are from the calculations of Raassen and Uylings 1998 made with the orthogonal operators method. These calculations did not include the 3d66p and several other highly excited configurations observed experimentally. Percentage compositions of the levels above 106000 cm-1 were determined by a parametric fitting of all known odd-parity levels with Cowan's codes (A. Kramida, unpublished, 2013). The additional quantum numbers of the repeating terms of the 3dn subshells, required to uniquely identify the quantum states, were also found in this calculation. These quantum numbers are the Nielson and Koster indexes tabulated by Martin et al. 1978 . In the parametric calculation, theoretical eigenstates were identified with observed energy levels by comparing the predicted and observed sets of transitions and their intensities. As a result, for 68 levels the configuration and term labels given by Nave and Johansson 2013 were changed in the present list.

    As noted by Sugar and Corliss 1985 , the 3d54s4p configuration is best described by the 3d5(S1L1)+4s4p(S2L2°) S3L3 coupling. Thus, the levels of this configuration are labeled in this coupling scheme. However, the percentage compositions of these levels are given in the 3d54s(S1L1)+4p(2P°) SL coupling as used by Raassen and Uylings 1998 . The 3d6nf,g,h configurations are best described in the jK coupling scheme, otherwise called J1l, in which the total angular momentum J1 of the core is coupled with the orbital momentum l of the valence electron to produce an intermediate momentum K, which finally couples with the spin of the valence electron to produce the total angular momentum J. These levels are labeled in this coupling scheme. However, the percentage compositions of the 3d64f configuration, quoted from Raassen and Uylings 1998 , are given in the LS coupling.

    Many energy levels have highly mixed compositions from different interacting configurations. For such levels, the configuration and term labels have little physical meaning. A somewhat better description is provided by the eigenvector compositions given here.

Fe II Energy Levels
Lines:  Nave and Johansson 2013     Nave and Johansson 2013 reported about 13,000 lines observed in emission from hollow Fe-Ne and Fe-Ar cathode discharge lamps. The observations were made using Fourier-transform spectrometers in a wide spectral range from near ultraviolet to far infrared, and with grating spectrographs for shorter wavelengths. In the present line list, the relative intensities reported by Nave and Johansson have been converted to the same uniform scale using the method described by Kramida 2013 . This intensity scale corresponds to emission from plasma roughly described by a local thermodynamic equilibrium with an effective temperature of 1.9 eV corresponding to the majority of observations of Nave and Johansson. It is well known that in hollow cathode discharges the populations of highly excited levels of analyzed atoms and ions admixed to the buffer rare gases are greatly enhanced by collisional population transfer from excited rare-gas atoms. Such enhancement of about one or two orders of magnitude was observed in the spectra reported by Nave and Johansson for Fe II energy levels above 90,000 cm-1. To make the intensity scale more uniform, this enhancement was removed from the intensities given here. Wavelength-dependent variations of efficiency of the registration equipment were removed from the intensity values. The intensities are given on an arbitrary linear scale proportional to the total energy flux under the line profile.

    The line list of Nave and Johansson 2013 is supplemented here with several tens of parity-forbidden lines observed or predicted to occur in astrophysical spectra, and with several tens of lines observed by Dobbie 1938 and by King 1938 . These authors used spark and arc light sources that had significantly lower excitation temperatures of about 1 eV. They observed many weak lines that could not be observed in higher-temperature hollow cathodes. The present list includes only those lines from Dobbie and King that were included in the list of lines with transition probabilities critically assessed by Fuhr and Wiese 2006 . Additional weak lines observed by Johansson et al. 1995 , Pickering et al. 2001 , Pickering et al. 2002 , Karlsson et al. 2001 , and by Schnabel et al. 2004 were included for the same reason. Their relative intensities, where available, were reduced to the same scale from the observed branching fractions reported by these authors. Due to the large variety of observational conditions and the large observed wavelength range, from 899 Å to 5 μm, the dynamic range of observed intensities is extremely large, 10 orders of magnitude. Because of this, intensities of the weakest lines are given in scientific (exponential) notation, e.g., 8.e-3.

    Johansson et al. 1995 have observed anomalously strong intercombination transitions involving the highly mixed w 2P°3/2 and x 6P°3/2 levels. It was shown by Donnelly and Hibbert 1998 that these intercombination lines gain their strength due to the mixing of these levels with the 3d6(3D)4p w 4P°3/2 level.

Fe II Line Wavelengths and Classification
Transition Probabilities:  Fuhr & Wiese 2006, Raassen & Uylings 1998, Deb & Hibbert 2011, Bautista et al. 2015, Tayal & Zatsarinny 2018, Mendoza et al. 2023, Rostohar et al. 2001, Hartman et al. 2003, Gurell et al. 2009     When Fuhr & Wiese made their critical compilation in 2006, they were restricted by 577 energy levels of Fe II available in ASD at that time. Since the current level list includes almost twice as many levels, it became possible to extend their set of recommended transition probabilities for allowed transitions by assessing the accuracy of many additional transitions calculated by Raassen & Uylings 1998 . This assessment was made using the method described by Kramida 2013 , resulting in inclusion of evaluated transition probabilities for more than 6000 transitions in addition to those included in Fuhr & Wiese 2006 . Some of their reported values have been revised by A. Kramida (unpublished, 2013) on the basis of new comparisons. In particular, allowed transition probabilities reported by Johansson et al. 1995 have been re-normalized using the calculations of Raassen & Uylings 1998 made with the orthogonal operators method. This update of the data set for allowed transitions was made by A. Kramida for the release of ASD v.5.1 in September, 2013. Since then, Uylings & Raassen made new extended calculations of allowed transitions with the same orthogonal-operators technique. Their new results are available in their database at https://personal.sron.nl/%7Etonr/atomphys/atomphys.html, but their accuracy remains to be assessed. For parity-forbidden transitions, the data set given by Fuhr & Wiese 2006 has been replaced here by an extended set of data critically evaluated by A. Kramida (unpublished) for the release of ASD v. 5.11 in November 2023. This data set includes all 678 transitions with estimated branching fractions greater than 0.001. Out of those, 120 had been observed (mostly in astrophysical objects), but no precise wavelength measurements are available for them, except for the two transitions at 51.3 μm and 87.4 μm, which were precisely measured by Brown et al. 1998 . For all other observed forbidden transitions, the wavelength given in the "Observed Wavelength" column is actually a rounded-off Ritz wavelength, which is indicated by the colon symbol (:) in the intensity column. Radiative lifetimes of several even-parity levels had been measured by Rostohar et al. 2001 , by Hartman et al. 2003 , and by Gurell et al. 2009 . The two latter papers also reported the measured branching fractions for radiative decay of the levels involved. In the present data set, these data were supplemented by relative intensities derived by Bautista et al. 2015 and by Mendoza et al. 2023 from astrophysical observations. Bautista et al. 2015 provided a set of recommended radiative lifetimes of metastable even-parity levels and branching fractions for transitions from them derived from several critically assessed theoretical calculations, the most accurate of which was made by Deb & Hibbert 2011 . Since that time, one more similarly accurate calculation has been published by Tayal & Zatsarinny 2018 . All these data for forbidden transitions were critically analyzed by A. Kramida (unpublished, November 2023) for the release of ASD v.5.11, leaving the data set for allowed transitions unchanged, as it was in ASD v.5.1 of 2013. Fe II Transition Probabilities
