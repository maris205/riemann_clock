## Issues resolved during review

- The abstract originally said that same-transition order differences “reveal sensitivity to” conventional instrumental or modelling effects. It now says they **motivate additional checks** of calibration, extraction and line-profile modelling. This describes the diagnostic without asserting an identified cause from a post-inspection conditional discrepancy.
- The limitations originally stated too broadly that original acquisition records were unavailable. It now acknowledges the retrieved S2D arrays and reduction logs and specifies what has not been reconstructed: full extraction covariance, independently calibrated response matrices and complete calibration budgets. That is consistent with the actual exposure-level work.
- The old rank-failure paragraph now begins “For the initial two-sightline pilot, ...”. This correctly prevents “only 1608 remains” from being mistaken for a statement about the six newly selected archive systems. The original two-sightline design-matrix degeneracy is not generalized to the expanded sample.

## Scientific boundaries that pass

- **Combined masks and covariance:** the new fits apply both changes together, on the same retained pixels and covariance within each H0/H1 pair. The text does not add or subtract improvements from different pixel selections as if they were one likelihood-ratio statistic. Retaining the supplied variance amplitude is clearly stated.
- **No causal attribution from masking:** both the text and information figure state that removing cores also removes velocity sensitivity. The illustrative direction was motivated by the already-seen strong-line pattern and is not an independent atomic prediction. The manuscript does not claim that saturation explains the whole offset, nor that local information loss exactly predicts the nonlinear objective reduction.
- **Gas architecture:** the fixed merger preserves initialization moments, not complete Voigt profiles. Sub-resolution structures are not declared unmeasurable. The 40/45-component comparisons include altered bounds and are not described as an ordinary nested test of the true cloud count. Changes of roughly 22 m/s are conditional model sensitivity, not additional physical evolution.
- **Exposure estimator comparability:** native-exposure means near −42/−46 m/s are compared with the combined spectrum evaluated by the same fixed-template estimator. The text explicitly distinguishes this from the full gas-refitting estimates near −112/−101 m/s. Agreement of representations of the same photons does not provide independent evidence. Shared-template uncertainty and diagonal extraction errors remain conditioning assumptions.
- **Same-transition order discrepancy:** the 2600 difference compares the same transition within exposures; a true astrophysical frequency does not depend on its detector order. The discrepancy usefully motivates conventional checks, but the text does not identify one mechanism, claim that all original offsets are explained, or promote its conditional error to a discovery significance. The width-bound limitation is retained.
- **Exposure time versus cosmic age:** the 2018 and 2019–2020 groups provide observational consistency checks with different photons but shared astrophysics and calibration assumptions. They are not independent cosmic epochs for this slowly evolving hypothesis. No age trend is inferred from the exposure grouping.
- **Archive scope:** 155 listed DLA systems, 36 initial candidates, 32 retrieved sightlines, 324 line windows, seven strict metadata candidates and six final quality candidates are distinct stages. The six are potential modelling targets, not six alpha measurements. The DLA screen is not a census of every metal absorber in all 467 spectra. The text preserves same-sightline calibration dependence, catalogue-redshift limitations and the need for gas/response modelling.
- **Calibration provenance:** no explicit nonzero shift/slope entries in the extra spectra's logs is treated as a provenance flag, not proof that every calibration correction is absent. This avoids assigning precision to general archival coadds that the present work has not established.
- **Overall hypothesis:** all current diagnostics can inform a conventional uncertainty budget, but neither a relative shift nor profile discrepancy has an established mapping to the proposed Riemann-zero uncertainty component. The explicit withholding of alpha and cosmic-time inference is scientifically justified.

With the wording clarifications resolved, this section supports the conclusion that existing data can substantially extend the tests while conventional instrumental and modelling explanations remain unresolved. It does not support a positive detection, a null constraint on the proposed cosmic amplitude, or preference for the inverse-log-squared exponent. Final numerical product checks remain the responsibility of the separately assigned reviews.
