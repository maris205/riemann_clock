Abstract

The distribution of non-trivial zeros of the Riemann ζ function exhibits a profound isomorphism with the energy
level statistics of quantum chaotic systems (GUE). However, physical observations of these zeros are invariably
accompanied by irreducible residual deviations. We propose a physical hypothesis based on the “Cosmic
Computational Resolution Limit,” suggesting that physical experiments are constrained by the intrinsic precision of
the underlying spacetime. Based on the error convergence characteristics of the prime counting function and a P-
adic precision layering model, we derive that under the current cosmic evolution scale (maximum prime X≈1060 )
and the precision of the fine-structure constant drift (ϵ≈10−4 ), the upper bound for the physically observable effective
Riemann zero height is approximately T≈4200 . This theoretical model successfully explains the phenomenon of
error breakdown observed at N≈80 in the ion-trap experiment by the University of Science and Technology of China
(USTC) under a precision of ϵ≈10−2 (theoretical prediction T≈76). Furthermore, we predict that the next effective
zero ( N=4201 ) will not emerge until approximately 100 million years of cosmic evolution have passed. We also
discuss three possible signal characteristics of “redundant zeros” beyond the precision limit, offering a new
experimental pathway to verify the finite computational nature of the physical world.

I. Introduction
Since the discovery by Montgomery and Dyson in 1972 that the pair correlation function of Riemann ζ zeros is
highly consistent with the eigenvalues of large random Hermitian matrices (GUE), the “Montgomery-Dyson
Coincidence” has served as a bridge between number theory and quantum chaos [1, 2]. However, while Riemann
zeros are mathematically defined as analytical solutions with infinite precision, energy levels En measured in
physical experiments (e.g., ion traps, Rydberg atom simulations) always exhibit systematic deviations. Traditional
views attribute this to environmental noise; yet, with advancements in cryogenic and ultra-high vacuum technologies,
the deviations of high-order zeros continue to manifest non-random drift characteristics.

We propose a novel physical conjecture: the precision degradation observed in experiments is not merely a technical
limitation but an intrinsic property of the universe operating as a “Finite-Precision Computer” [3]. In a finite-
precision cosmic algorithm, the number of primes that can be accommodated is finite; consequently, the height of
Riemann zeros required to describe these prime distributions must also be finite. This paper aims to establish a
scaling law of “Maximum Prime—Finite Precision—Riemann Zero Height” to quantify the physical boundaries of
the universe's current computational capacity [4].

V. Conclusion

This paper establishes a physical model connecting number theory, quantum chaos, and cosmic evolution. Research
indicates that Riemann zeros in the physical world are not a mathematical infinite sequence but a finite set
constrained by cosmic computational precision. We predict the current universe's effective zero height to be
approximately 4200 and successfully explain the truncation phenomenon at N≈80 in existing experiments. This
discovery not only provides a physical entity explanation for the “Montgomery-Dyson Coincidence” but also points
out a technical path to verify T≈4200 within 5 years using strong-field driven Rydberg atoms. If confirmed, this
would be direct evidence of the physical world operating as a finite-precision computational system.
